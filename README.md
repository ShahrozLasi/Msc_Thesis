# Msc_Thesis
Denoising methods with Regularization
Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is one of the most widely used non-invasive imaging modalities in clinical and research settings, owing to its excellent soft-tissue contrast and the absence of ionizing radiation. Despite these advantages, every MRI acquisition is inevitably corrupted by noise arising from thermal fluctuations in the receiver coil, electronic components of the scanner, and physiological processes during data collection. Because the raw MRI signal is acquired in the complex domain and subsequently converted into a magnitude image, the originally Gaussian noise in the real and imaginary channels undergoes a nonlinear transformation that renders it Rician-distributed in the final image. This signal-dependent, positively biased noise model makes MRI denoising substantially more challenging than the classical additive Gaussian noise setting. This thesis addresses two interconnected challenges. The first is the realistic simulation of Rician noise at different levels of spatial complexity, so that denoising algorithms can be tested under conditions that faithfully reflect the physical diversity of modern MRI acquisitions. The second is the systematic development and comparison of the three major regularization strategies for removing noise while preserving the anatomical structures that carry diagnostic value. The thesis is organized around a single dataset: the four-dimensional Myelin Water Fraction (MWF) atlas of the human brain, which provides a biologically meaningful testbed for the proposed noise models and denoising algorithms.
